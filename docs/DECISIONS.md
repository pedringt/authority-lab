# Decisions and roadmap

The product decisions behind Authority Lab, why they were made, and what's next. Newest decisions are at the end of each section. When a PR changes or implements one of these, update this file in the same PR.

## Product reset direction — 2026-10-14

Authority Lab is in a product reset, not a code reset. The reset docs are the current source of truth for product direction:

- `docs/PRODUCT_MODEL.md`
- `docs/PRODUCT_PRINCIPLES.md`
- `docs/SETUP_FLOW.md`
- `docs/V1_SCOPE.md`
- `docs/TEMPLATES.md`
- `docs/AI_ASSISTANCE.md`
- `docs/GLOSSARY.md`
- `docs/IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md`

Where those docs conflict with older **interface/setup wording**, the reset direction wins. Existing governance/safety invariants remain in force unless Paige explicitly changes them.

Current explicit changes include:
- user-facing **Boundaries** replaces Rules as the main concept/section;
- user-facing **Requirements** replaces Decision requirements;
- Targets are required only when a Requirement needs a measurable bar;
- People are presented primarily as Owner / Reviewers / Stakeholders, with complex approval policy underneath;
- existing capabilities get a current-state/effective-authority baseline;
- Coverage sufficiency comes from explicit rules/deterministic checks, while AI may only flag blind spots;
- Residual Risk is reviewed at Proposal/Decision time;
- material changes may make prior Evidence less applicable and trigger re-evaluation;
- every core workflow needs a lightweight/manual path for smaller organizations;
- generative UI is a v1 presentation requirement, but it cannot alter deterministic authority rules;
- visual redesign is paused until the product model and information architecture stabilize.

Earlier redesign mockups and interface directions remain historical context only. Do not implement them as the current target unless Paige explicitly re-approves them.

## Principle

**AI interprets. Software enforces configured controls. Humans authorize.** The AI reads cases and recommends. Where a control is configured, software applies the limits and can pull authority back automatically; written Boundaries are followed by people. Only a named person can give the AI more authority.

**Positioning:** State governs what's true; Authority Lab governs what the AI may do.

## Decisions

### Phase 6 scope, and 6A.1: the Decision workspace (Paige, 2026-10-21)

Phase 6 was scoped read-only first, against the small firm, State and an aggressive client, on three candidates: the Decision workspace, Context intake and description understanding. The question was whether adaptive presentation makes the work substantially easier without making any consequential decision easier to bypass. Paige's decisions:

1. **Plan both paths.** **Phase 6A** is deterministic adaptive UI inside the current static app: sequencing, progressive disclosure, relevance-based sections, local rules, clearer questions. **Phase 6B** adds a model layer later, as progressive enhancement, once its architecture is decided (where it runs, provider, privacy boundary, cost, fallback, and the "no network or model calls" rule in `CLAUDE.md`). Both sit on the same deterministic records and governance rules; 6B enhances interpretation and presentation and never becomes a second authority system. A model may turn free-form Context into proposed facts, spot ambiguity and ask, suggest a work type, arrange the Decision workspace around what matters in the case (within the invariants), and draft summaries. It never decides the Authority level, whether a hard Requirement is Met, whether Coverage is sufficient, whether a Boundary is enforced, who may approve, or whether expansion is permitted. 6A is the fallback whenever the model is unavailable, disabled, too uncertain, or not wanted. The deciding evidence: today's description matcher reads "a partner reviews and sends them" and "it never sends anything" both as Acts, and misses "confidential client tax records"; rules can improve, but they aren't a long-term substitute for reading meaning.
2. **Defaults in the Decision workspace are suggestions only.** Each is accepted or edited explicitly, one field at a time, and the record notes which suggested values were accepted unchanged. Residual Risk and the person's reason never get defaults. In scope: rollout owner = the current Owner, start date = today, scope = the capability's summary, review date = a valid suggested date, outcomes proposed from existing measured Targets.
3. **Decision requirements don't change in Phase 6.** A move to Draft still needs 2–4 outcomes and the full rollout plan. First measure how much of the friction goes away through presentation, defaults, grouping and progressive disclosure; level-scaled requirements come back only as a separate product-rule decision if Draft still feels disproportionately heavy.
4. **Clear / Likely / Unsure on both paths.** In 6A the label reflects how decisive the deterministic rule was, and ambiguous cases are Unsure; in 6B, how strongly a model supports the reading. The source phrase is always shown. The label is never called "confidence" and never shown as a number or an objective score. (Applies to interpreted facts in 6A.2 and later, not to the 6A.1 defaults.)

**6A.1, the Decision workspace (#161):**
- **Only what the choice needs is asked.** Residual Risk is asked once the person chooses to expand, as its own step after "Your decision"; Hold, Reduce, Pause and Redesign ask for a reason only. The Risk step still shows the initial Risk and the controls. A Residual Risk already given always shows, whatever is chosen, so nothing recorded is out of sight.
- **"Where this stands":** one list under the heading that answers what stands between the decision and an expansion: approved and proposed Authority, operating above approved Authority, a pending post-incident review, hard Requirements not Met (each with its result), considerations open, no hard quality Requirement, Reviewer positions missing, a Reviewer saying Hold or Restrict, positions that differ, a stale Proposal. Each links to where it is resolved. It is a summary only: every full notice still shows, the checklist before the record button stays, and `canAuthorize` stays the only judge.
- **Suggested values,** labelled "Authority Lab suggestion" with their basis, under the rollout owner, start date, review date (30 days after the start, inside the 90-day limit) and scope, and a list of outcome suggestions from measured Targets whose bar has a number. "Use this" fills one field; an outcome goes in the first free row, and a duplicate or a full table is refused. A value edited after accepting is the person's own. The record lists, under "Accepted suggestions", only those left unchanged, frozen like the rest of the record; a published record carries the list and replay keeps only what the replayed values bear out. No suggestion exists for the feedback channel, what reviewers need to know, Residual Risk or the reason.
- **"Show every field"** (`?tab=decide&fields=all`) asks everything up front, as before 6A: Residual Risk inside the Risk step, and the outcomes and rollout plan whenever the open Proposal is one level up.
- **Invariants contract test** (`scripts/phase6a.test.mjs`): for a hard Requirement not met, an Unknown judgment, an open consideration, no hard quality Requirement, a missing Reviewer position, a Reviewer saying Hold, a stale Proposal and operating above approved Authority, the Decision workspace shows the case and what is newly permitted, still needs a person and stays prohibited, the controls, the evidence, the positions and the record button, with nothing chosen, Hold or Expand, with and without "Show every field"; Residual Risk is asked wherever it could be needed and never prefilled.
- Implementation call to check: the suggested review date is 30 days after the start (`SUGGESTED_REVIEW_DAYS`). Any valid date can be chosen.
- Follow-up (#163, after the production re-walk): "Where this stands" shows operating above approved Authority as a warning when the open Proposal governs the observed behavior (only going beyond the observed level is blocked); the record lists an accepted outcome once ("Expected outcome: Quality: ≥ 92% correct decisions"); an unknown suggestion key is refused as "There is no suggestion for that." Open for Paige: the band names a Reviewer who says Hold or Restrict, but a Stakeholder's opposing position shows only as "Positions differ".

### Remediation after the deep review (Paige, 2026-10-20)

After Phase 5, a read-only whole-product review walked three scenarios (State, a small team, the Northstar demo). Paige's decisions from it:

- **D1.** Declared vs actual behavior is a first-class concept.
- **D2.** Real workspaces can progress beyond Draft.
- **D3.** Hard Requirements block expansion; considerations don't.
- **D4.** Risk shapes the questions, not domain-specific controls. No financial → money-threshold rule.
- **D5.** A self-declared Low Risk can't erase safeguards.
- **D6.** Fix small-team governance.
- **D7.** Written vs enforced Boundaries are never conflated.
- **D8.** Requirements and Targets stay separate in the model but merge in the user experience.

Mechanics:

- **R1-B.** "Operating above approved Authority" is one of the most serious Monitor states. It blocks expansion until resolved, and never blocks Reduce, Pause, Retire or correcting behavior. Monitor shows the observed behavior, the approved Authority, that further expansion is blocked, and two ways to resolve it: bring behavior back within approved Authority, or govern the higher behavior through Proposal and Decision.
- **R2-A.** One level at a time. The gap stays visible until declared catches up or behavior comes down.
- **R3-A** (PR 2). Mark Northstar's high-value sample row as a consideration.
- **R4-A** (PR 2). A Proposal can be opened while hard Requirements are unresolved; the expansion Decision is what's blocked.
- **R5-B** (PR 3). Low stays the human's label, but confirmed facts drive the baseline safeguards, with a clear mismatch warning.
- **R6** (PR 4). After results exist, these need sign-off: lowering or loosening a Target or bar; removing a hard Requirement; changing hard → consideration; changing an existing evidence mapping or source; a Boundary change that permits more; removing a Reviewer; changing Reviewer ↔ Stakeholder role. These are recorded only: linking a previously unlinked Requirement or Target to evidence; adding a Reviewer or Stakeholder; tightening a bar; adding a Requirement; adding or tightening a Boundary.

### Product reset, Phase 5: Monitor and lifecycle (#147, Paige, 2026-10-19)

The settled model (Paige, 2026-10-19): Monitor answers "Is the current Authority still justified?" Monitoring may trigger attention or another human decision; it never increases or restores Authority. A material change means evidence may need revalidating and a person should look, not that software decides authority is unsafe. Increasing Authority needs evidence and approval; reducing or pausing it should be easier. Authority is never restored automatically: recovery makes a new Proposal possible. Automatic restrictions stay exactly deterministic.

As built:

- **Monitor's first question** (`justification`, `src/store/lifecycle.js`; the **Still justified?** view). It has three parts:
  - **Current state:** Authority, the Decision in force, Boundaries, and any automatic restriction or review lock.
  - **What changed since the Decision in force:** compared with its frozen snapshot (#144). Covers Requirement and Target results, coverage states, positions, new evidence, stale evidence, disagreement, material changes, incidents (lightweight and rule), restriction rules over their limit, attempts at something never allowed, people's approvals and overrides, rollout goals missed on real numbers, and adoption warnings.
  - **A plain-language state, no score:** Current Authority still supported / Needs attention / Investigation needed / Authority may no longer be justified / Restricted automatically. The state is the most serious change found, and each change says why.

  The automatic safety limits, the evidence signals and the gate summary follow, unchanged.
- **Signal to state (the mapping used, for Paige to confirm):**
  - "May no longer be justified": a result that was Met when decided is now Not met.
  - "Investigation needed": a result is now Conflicting, a critical incident is open, a rule incident awaits review, or a restriction rule reads over its limit.
  - "Needs attention": any other change for the worse, stale evidence, a material change, a position change or disagreement, an attempt at something never allowed, a rollout goal missed on real numbers, or an adoption warning.
  - Information only: new evidence, improvements, people's approvals and overrides.
  - "Restricted automatically": an active automatic restriction.
- **No snapshot, no result comparison.** The starting record, or a Decision made before #144, has nothing to compare with. Requirements are the bar for the next level, so one not met now says nothing about the current Authority by itself; only signals that stand on their own count.
- **Material-change revalidation is date-based and never fails anything** (`materialChangeImpact`). For each change since the Decision, Monitor lists every Requirement and Target whose current result rests on evidence observed before the change took effect: "may need new evidence", or "partly after" once newer evidence exists. No category-to-source invalidation is applied: none is configured. Imported evidence measured under another configuration is already set aside by its own standing. Authority never changes. Records now freeze `materialChangesAtDecision`, so Monitor can tell which changes came after. A material change can be recorded from Monitor by the Owner or anyone named, the Phase 1 rule.
- **Pulling back from Monitor.** Reduce one level, Suspend or Redesign can be recorded without an open Proposal, by anyone named, with a reason (Phase 4 Q1). The store always allowed this; there was no screen for it. Restore is a new Proposal, one level at a time, and the Owner's Decision; never automatic after better evidence, a resolved incident or a review. After a decision is recorded, Decide now offers **Propose a move to Level N** beneath it when a Proposal could be made (the existing rule: up to Draft, with evidence, no review pending); before, the recorded view never offered it again.
- **Overrides as monitoring evidence**, from the existing gate log:
  - attempts at something never allowed (always blocked) need attention;
  - people's approvals of needs-a-person actions, people's overrides (rejections) and other blocked actions are information.

  No new exception or override record was added.

Paige's answers (2026-10-19, PR #148 review):

- **Q1. Material-change applicability: A.** Keep the conservative, date-based "May need new evidence". No category-to-evidence dependency map until there is structured dependency information.
- **Q2. Monitor states: confirmed as built** (mapping above). No score.
- **Q3. Reviewer disagreement stays "Needs attention".** Important and visible, but not "Investigation needed" and not a veto, unless there is evidence or an incident behind it.
- **Q4. Pause = the Suspend Decision, called Pause.** It moves to Level 0 through the existing Suspend mechanics (the internal option stays `suspend`). It needs a reason and takes an optional planned review date (`setPauseReviewDate`, frozen on the record as `plannedReviewDate`). The date is a reminder only: nothing changes on it. There is no special temporary level and no shortcut back; getting authority back takes current evidence and a new Proposal and Decision. The status reads "Paused". "Pause and redesign" was renamed "Send back for redesign" so Pause has one meaning.
- **Q5. Restore = the normal Proposal path, with restoration context.** One level at a time, same rules. When the capability is below a level it had before a reduction, Pause or automatic restriction, the Proposal freezes `restoration` (`restorationContext`): earlier Authority, why it came down and by whom, the review and incidents around it, what has changed since, and new evidence. Decide shows "Restore toward Level N"; after a reduction the button reads "Propose: restore toward Level N" (or "Propose Level 1: restoring toward Level 2" when more than one step remains).
- **Q6. Retire: Owner only, reason required, terminal** (`retireCapability`). It writes a record (`option: 'retire'`, with the frozen `reviewed` snapshot), moves to Observe with status "retired", closes any open Proposal, and keeps all history and records. No Decision or Proposal can follow (`RETIRED_REASON`), so there is no Restore. To use the work again, set up a new capability. Kept distinct from "Not delegated, by design", which means it was never delegated. On screen, Retired overrides the authority label (Paige, 2026-10-20): it is stored at Level 0, but lists, the Status header, Monitor and the retirement record read "Retired", never "Observe", which would suggest it still sees requests. A paused capability still reads "Observe · Paused".
- **Q7. Investigate stays an activity for v1:** Monitor's links to evidence, results, judgments and incidents. A formal investigation record may come after the deep review.
- **Q8. Exceptions: deliberately deferred.** No exception record or approval/duration rules. The settled principles stand:
  - must-never/prohibited Boundaries are never excepted;
  - exceptions, if built, are narrow and temporary;
  - repeated exceptions should become a Monitor signal;
  - existing human approvals, overrides and blocked attempts stay monitoring evidence and History.

  Unresolved, for the deep review: how an approved exception would interact with the runtime gate. Authority Lab must not record an "approved exception" that enforcement doesn't honor.
- **Q9. No Monitor state creates a hard block.** Only the existing deterministic mechanisms block: automatic restriction, the post-incident review lock, Boundary enforcement and the Proposal/Decision rules.

### Product reset, Phase 4: Proposal and Decision (#144, Paige, 2026-10-18)

The settled model (Paige, 2026-10-18): a Proposal is one concrete proposed authority change that a person explicitly creates; it answers "What changes if I approve this?" and is not a Decision. A Decision is the recorded human choice. Evidence never grants authority; AI may prepare the workspace but never records the Decision. Residual Risk is a person's judgment (Low / Moderate / High / Unknown with a reason), never a score. Reviewer disagreement is visible, never averaged away. Every existing approval and expansion rule is kept.

What follows directly from that, as built:

- **The Proposal is frozen when a person proposes** (`proposeAuthority` → `freezeProposal`, `src/store/proposal.js`). It records who proposed it (`proposedBy`, `proposedByAt`) and freezes current and proposed authority; newly permitted (the proposed level's plain description and the Rules' "may" lines), still needs a person (the level's human step, "must ask" and escalation lines) and still prohibited ("must never" lines); every Boundary with its enforcement; Requirement and Target results with their reasons; coverage; Reviewer and Stakeholder positions; the initial Risk; versions, the Context and baseline versions, the material changes known, and imported-evidence provenance. Proposals are append-only (`capabilityData.authorityProposals`, ids `AP-NN`); open, decided or closed is derived, never written onto the frozen object.
- **The Decision record freezes what the person deciding saw.** It adds the Proposal reviewed (`proposalId`), whether it was stale (`proposalStale`, with reasons), and `reviewed`: Requirement and Target results with reasons, coverage, Boundaries with enforcement, the initial Risk, every position, and the evidence behind each result (below). A decision opened before Proposals were frozen (Northstar's seeded pending decision) is recorded with `proposalLegacy: true`; no snapshot is invented for it.
- **Kept unchanged:** one level at a time; expansion needs a pending proposal; evidence needed before proposing, real-use evidence for Level 3; setup confirmation; the post-incident review lock and its independence rule; automatic restrictions; reductions close an incompatible open proposal; expected impact and a rollout plan for every expansion; `canAuthorize` as the only judge of whether a decision can be recorded; immutable records; evidence and version snapshots; the decision brief; and the bar-change sign-off rules (frozen approvers, feasibility, Risk approvers, proposer never approves).

Paige's answers (2026-10-19, PR #145 review):

- **Q1. Who records the Decision: asymmetric.** Expand (either kind) and Keep current limits: only the capability's Owner (`OWNER_DECIDES`, `decisionEligibility`). Restrict, Suspend and Redesign: anyone active and named on the capability (the Owner, a Reviewer or a Stakeholder). Increasing or affirmatively keeping authority is the Owner's accountable decision; pulling it back stays easier. The post-incident review is a separate record and keeps its rules; automatic restrictions are unchanged. Anyone active may still propose.
- **Q2. Reviewer input is required, not a veto.** Before an expansion every active named Reviewer must have given a position (`reviewerInputMissing`); Stakeholder input stays optional. A Reviewer saying Hold or Restrict is shown prominently and doesn't block, since no configured approval policy says it does (none was invented). Hold and reductions don't wait for Reviewers.
- **Q3. Staleness is decision-relevant, blocks expansion, and is fixed by replacement.** A Proposal is stale when, since it was frozen, a versioned object it was reviewed against (Rules, Targets, Requirements, Risk) has a newer version; who is named on the capability, or as what (Reviewer or Stakeholder), changes; a material change is recorded; a Requirement or Target result changes (any change of status: Met to Not met, Unknown to Met…); or a row's coverage changes state (sufficient, insufficient, stale, conflicting, missing, Unknown, needs a decision; `coverageState`). More evidence that leaves every result and coverage state as it was doesn't count. A stale Proposal blocks Expand; it doesn't block Keep current, Restrict, Suspend or Redesign. It is never refreshed in place: `replaceProposal` freezes a new Proposal (`replaces: <old id>`) from current conditions, and an append-only closure (`proposalClosures`) records that the old one was replaced because it was stale, with the reasons, who and when. History lists every Proposal and what became of it. A Reviewer or Stakeholder giving or changing a position, stance or reasoning after the Proposal opened doesn't make it stale (Paige, 2026-10-19, follow-up): that is reviewing the Proposal, not changing what it describes. The Proposal keeps the positions it was frozen with, the Decide flow shows current input beside them, and the Decision record freezes the positions in force when it is recorded.
- **Q4. Residual Risk is required for every expansion.** Low / Moderate / High / Unknown plus a short human reason (at least `RESIDUAL_REASON_MIN`, 10 characters). Unknown and High are valid; software never decides whether the risk is acceptable. Not required for Hold or reductions. Where no person can judge one, only in the replay of already recorded fictional agent runs and the demo-only `agent --expand` helper, Unknown is recorded with a note saying no one judged it: those are compatibility paths, not authority decisions. The guided demo teaches the real rule (Paige, 2026-10-19, follow-up): the viewer must choose a level, Unknown included, and give a reason before it records, and opening a later step directly lands on the decision step instead of deciding for them.
- **Q5. No software-written reason.** `proposeAuthority` starts the reason empty, and it is emptied again after every decision. Northstar's seeded wording stays, labelled "Prefilled for the demo"; a record that keeps it unedited says so (`rationalePrefilled`).
- **Evidence provenance is frozen for every path (completeness fix).** `evidenceProvenance` freezes, for every Requirement and Target, the results that judged it and the items behind each (every item of a combined result), with source, type, date, metric and value, who recorded or imported it, upload file details, the human judgments that decided a person-judged row, recorded test outcomes when the tests source judged it, open incidents that blocked it, and the Target a Requirement is judged through. Items, judgments, outcomes and incidents are listed once by id rather than copying the evidence store. Imported evidence keeps its `externalSnapshot` too. The record's "Evidence at the time" shows this when the old summary is empty, so a decision on evidence recorded by hand never shows an empty section; the brief does the same.
- **The decision brief in a real workspace** names the workspace; "fictional demo data" appears only in the Northstar demo.
- Out of scope, still open: the double full stop in some judgment reasons.

### Product reset, Phase 3: the lightweight evidence path (#142, Paige, 2026-10-16)

The question: can a small team with no governance, eval, observability or data stack collect enough credible evidence inside Authority Lab to support an authority decision? Manual first never means manual only: State's committed-file path stays, and both paths converge on one evidence model. Authority Lab stays eval-aware, not eval-centric: no eval runner, no model calls.

- **P1. Test cases record real outcomes.** A test case is a scenario the team means to evaluate. They run it through their real AI outside Authority Lab and **Record outcome**: Pass / Fail / Unable to judge, severity if it failed, what actually happened, an optional reference, and who recorded it. Simulated results are demo-only, clearly labelled as simulation, and never count as real Evidence or trigger the results lock in a real workspace. Simulation must never support a real authority decision.
- **P2. A person can judge a Requirement.** (Decided as "a Requirement that has no metric". Since the #143 review, which Requirements a person judges is stored on each Requirement, never inferred from its wording or from whether evidence is configured: see the implementation calls below.) A recorded judgment: Met / Not met / Unknown, with a short rationale, who, when, and an optional supporting reference. The Owner or a Reviewer may judge, including the Owner on their own capability (no artificial separation for a small team; an approval policy can still demand an independent Reviewer for the authority Decision). Stakeholders may give input, which never satisfies a Requirement by itself. A judgment is Evidence, not the Decision.
- **P3. Browser-local CSV/JSON upload.** Allowed for the lightweight path; the screen says plainly that it stays in that browser. It goes through the same envelope and checks as imported evidence where possible. Both per-case rows (aggregated by Authority Lab) and already-aggregated results are accepted, in no particular vendor's format. Provenance: file name, who imported it, when, a source label, and any dates and case counts present. No shared storage or backend.
- **P4. Compatible results combine.** Results combine only under explicit deterministic rules: the same Requirement/Target, metric, segment/population, evidence mode where relevant, and materially compatible configuration/context, all inside the freshness window. Three compatible manual reviews of 10 cases give 30 cases of Coverage. Disagreement is never averaged away, the underlying results stay as they were, and incompatible results don't combine. This replaces "newest silently wins" on the lightweight path.
- **P5. Conflicting.** A Requirement/Target is Conflicting when at least two current, applicable results independently judge it and disagree (one supports Met, another Not met). Conflicting never counts as Pass, isn't resolved by recency, shows which evidence disagrees, and asks a person to investigate or collect more. Stale, out-of-segment, explicitly superseded or otherwise non-applicable evidence creates no conflict. Ambiguous cases go back to Paige rather than into a precedence system.
- **P6. Manual incidents.** What happened, severity, date, capability, status (Open / Under review / Resolved), who recorded it, optional reference. An incident is Evidence and a lifecycle event, but not a numeric result unless explicitly mapped into a metric. An open critical incident makes "No unresolved critical incidents" Not met; resolving it removes that blocker, never erases the incident from History, and doesn't prove the Requirement met if other evidence is required.
- **P7. Feedback is context by default.** Source or person, date, note, optional sentiment, category or reference. It never changes readiness unless a Requirement is explicitly configured to be judged by it (a user-acceptance threshold, a reviewer approval). P2 judgments are different: they directly judge a non-metric Requirement.
- **P8. Minimal manual Impact actuals.** Metric or name, an actual value or qualitative result, measured vs estimated, period or date, who recorded it, optional note or reference. Impact stays informational and separate from readiness; high Impact never compensates for failed Requirements or Risk. No Impact dashboards or ROI modelling.
- **Words.** "Record outcome" for one test case; "Record evidence" or "Record result" for a metric or Requirement-level entry, so one case never sounds like an aggregate.
- **Implementation calls in #142, for review.** (1) Recorded outcomes (pass or fail) and Owner/Reviewer judgments of Met or Not met count as results and lock the bar; Unable to judge, Unknown judgments, feedback and Impact don't. A recorded incident doesn't either (Paige, 2026-10-17, #143 review): it is evidence and a lifecycle event, a numeric result only when explicitly mapped into a metric. (2) Only a **critical** incident blocks "No unresolved critical incidents"; Open and Under review both count as unresolved. (3) The Owner or a Reviewer changes an incident's status; anyone named on the capability may record one. (4) How a Requirement is judged is stored on it as `judgment`: `person` (an Owner or Reviewer decides) or `evidence` (a measure or linked Target shows it) (Paige, 2026-10-17, #143 review). It is never inferred from the wording or from whether evidence is configured yet: "All 4 templates have been approved" can be `person`; "Overall accuracy ≥ 90%" is `evidence` even before a source is chosen, and until then it reads No evidence yet. A coverage rule and a Requirement with linked Targets are always judged by evidence (a person-judged Requirement can't have a Target). Suggested Requirements get it when suggested: a coverage rule or one with a suggested Target is `evidence`, anything else `person`; the earlier risk-based defaults set it too (the incidents check is `person`, with the open-critical-incident blocker on top). A new hand-written Requirement on a setup-flow capability must answer "How will you know this is true?". Choose evidence configures an evidence-judged Requirement and is refused for a person-judged one; changing how a Requirement is judged is an edit to the Requirement, so after results it goes to sign-off. **Legacy Requirements (Paige, 2026-10-17).** A Requirement saved before `judgment` existed is read from its stored structure only: a coverage rule, a linked Target, a number still to decide, or a measure means evidence-judged. Anything else is a *legacy* row, because its intended judgment can't be recovered safely from what is stored. A legacy row's stored result stands exactly as before (met, or not met with its stored value and reason). Nobody records a judgment or a row result against it until someone says how it is judged. Its evidence can still be chosen, as before Phase 3. It's never listed as needing a link. No legacy row is rewritten. In the seeded demo there are 18: four each on Ticket classification, Response drafting and Account closure, and six on Refund execution > $50 (for example "Overall accuracy ≥ 85%", "No unresolved critical incidents", "A 30-day restore for closed accounts exists in production"). State has none: its Requirements are measured. A new Requirement, or a legacy one whose wording is edited, must answer "How will you know this is true?". Giving a legacy row its judgment, or changing one, after results is a governed change (sign-off). Removing a Requirement's Targets leaves its stored judgment as it was. (5) Combining applies to results recorded by hand or uploaded, within the manual source's 90-day window; a single value never combines; connected sources keep newest-run-per-configuration. (6) Feedback mapped to a row is read as real use, so it waits for the usual 20 entries. (7) Recording a result never turns a person-judged Requirement into a measured one. An evidence-judged Requirement with no evidence chosen yet, and a Target, are linked to results people record here (under the row's own metric) by their first result, before any results exist; Choose evidence starts on that same choice. A Requirement linked to Targets takes their result (approved by Paige, 2026-10-17): any linked Target Not met makes it Not met; otherwise any Conflicting makes it Conflicting; all Met makes it Met; otherwise Not enough evidence. After results, linking needs approval, so evidence should be chosen before the first result. Before the first result, every screen that records one lists the evidence-judged rows with no evidence linked (never a person-judged Requirement) and says linking them later may need approval (Paige, #143 review); the approval rule itself is unchanged.
- **The small-team path, end to end:** define Requirements and Targets; write test cases; use the real AI outside Authority Lab; record outcomes; record a human judgment on each person-judged Requirement; record an incident if one happens; optionally upload a CSV/JSON export or enter a production metric; Authority Lab works out Result and Coverage deterministically, including combined compatible evidence and conflicts; and the person can see exactly why each Requirement is Met, Not met, Unknown, Conflicting or Not enough evidence.

### Product reset, Phase 2: the setup flow (#139, Paige, 2026-10-16)

- **Setup is a draft until a person confirms it.** Each step is saved as it's done, so nothing is lost, with versions where they're useful. Adding a capability is not its first authority decision: a new capability is an unconfirmed draft with no granted authority (the gate treats it as Level 0: everything blocked) and no decision record. **Review and confirm** formally sets Level 0 or 1 and writes the first decision record and an immutable **Setup confirmed** snapshot of what the person reviewed, at the same time and by the same person. That keeps "every authority level change writes a decision record": it's written when authority is actually granted. Capabilities that existed before Phase 2 (Northstar's, State's, and any added through the old path) count as already established, on their existing history. No historical record is rewritten or manufactured to fit the new flow.
- **Confirmation gates authority, not exploration.** Before confirming, people may save setup, write test cases, run tests and configure or import evidence. They may not propose or grant more authority until setup is confirmed. Evidence collected before confirmation stays real evidence with its provenance; the later Proposal/Decision judges whether it applies.
- **Seeing results still locks the bar, even in a draft (C1).** The first recorded test result or imported/measured evidence locks Requirements, Targets and people under the existing rules (changes need sign-off), even while setup is unconfirmed. Setup warns clearly before that line is crossed. "Draft" never means it's safe to tune the bar after seeing the answers.
- **Templates: all eight families plus Start from scratch,** on one generic mechanism with no per-template flows: Knowledge / Research, Draft / Create, Extract / Classify, Recommend, Customer / User Interaction, Workflow Automation, Action / Transaction, Monitor / Detect. Every suggested Boundary, from the template or the risk answers, is reviewed one at a time (edit, confirm or reject). No confirm-all for consequential configuration.
- **Templates suggest the type, never the number (answer 4, C3).** Default Targets and measurable Requirements are suggested as a measure with the bar **Needs decision**; the number comes from a person. That includes the minimum-cases coverage Requirement: it stays required, but its number starts as Needs decision, and coverage can't be sufficient on it until a person sets one. Non-measurable Requirements need no Target.
- **Suggestions say where they came from.** "Template suggestion", "Based on your answers" or "Authority Lab suggestion"; never "Suggested by AI" for deterministic output. "AI suggestion" is kept for something a model actually generated.
- **An already-in-use capability needs its baseline before confirmation (Paige, 2026-10-16, #141 review).** What it does today, where people are involved, its permissions and actions, and its safeguards must each be recorded; "Unknown" is a valid entry. Effective authority may stay unknown or not recorded (shown as open). The point is to document what is and isn't known about the real system before Authority Lab confirms the setup. A planned capability needs no baseline.
- **Exposure stays in the Risk step,** because approval rules and Boundary suggestions depend on it. **Declared starting authority is never above Level 1**; an already-running capability's effective authority is shown separately, and formalizing it is a later, explicit authority decision.
- **The sequence is conceptual, not one page per step.** Related steps may share a screen as long as the order and review boundaries stay clear. A materially different information architecture needs Paige first.

### Product reset, Phase 1: the data model (#137, 2026-10-14)

Phase 1 makes the store able to represent the reset. No setup UI, Proposal/Decision or Monitor redesign; old records are never rewritten, and nothing new is invented for Northstar. Paige's answers on 2026-10-14:

- **Targets are optional (1A).** A capability may have Requirements and no Targets; a save with none records `noTargets` on the version, so "saved" stays explicit. Quality and Serious-error are no longer required rows; removing them is a recorded deviation that needs a reason, like removing any default. The minimum-cases row stays required and is classed as a **coverage rule** (`isCoverageRule`), not a Target.
- **"Needs decision" is unresolved, never pass or fail (5f).** A Target may say "Needs decision" instead of a number (`bar: 'needs-decision'`). It can't carry a measure. A Target may name the Requirement it judges (`requirement`); while its bar needs a decision that Requirement is not met and `unresolved`, so it still blocks readiness, and it says "no bar yet: it needs a decision. This isn't a failed result." Replacing a default number with "Needs decision" needs a reason. After results, setting the number (or clearing one to "Needs decision") is never tightening-only, so it goes through sign-off.
- **Overall Risk is derived (2B).** Severity is the existing "how serious" answer (`impact`, not renamed). Likelihood (Low / Moderate / High / Unknown, R2) and Scale (One case / Many cases / Systemic / Unknown, R3) are new optional fields on the versioned risk profile. Severity keeps its stored values (Low / Medium / High, logic keys) and reads as Low / Moderate / High. Overall Risk (Low / Moderate / High / Unknown) comes from the standard 3x3 (R1, `OVERALL_RISK_TABLE` in `src/store/risk.js`): Low Severity gives Low / Low / Moderate for Low / Moderate / High Likelihood; Moderate gives Low / Moderate / High; High gives Moderate / High / High. High Severity with Low Likelihood is still Moderate, so Severity alone never decides it. Every result says how it was reached. Unknown Likelihood gives Unknown overall Risk, never Low. Existing profiles read as Likelihood Unknown and Scale not recorded.
- **People (3B).** One Owner; each named entry is a Reviewer (`role: 'reviewer'`) or a Stakeholder (no role). Existing entries stay Stakeholders; Reviewers start empty. The approval rules underneath are unchanged and still read the whole named list. A role change is governed like a membership change: after results it goes to sign-off.
- **Planned vs in use, declared vs effective authority (4A).** Planned/in use comes from "What does it do today?" (nothing: planned; recommends, drafts, acts: in use); no answer, or "not something to delegate", is not recorded. Effective authority is only ever what a person recorded (`recordBaseline`), never inferred; until then it reads "not recorded". Northstar records none. State records effective Draft from its configuration (it prepares every proposal and a person decides each one in Review, docs above under "What State is"); its declared level stays Recommend.
- **Context, baseline and material changes (5a-5c).** Context belongs to the workflow (`state.contextVersions`); each fact is suggested, reviewed or confirmed, one at a time, and only confirmed facts may drive setup. The capability baseline (what it does today, human involvement, permissions, safeguards, evidence, known issues, effective authority) is versioned per capability. Material changes (model, prompt, tools, permissions, workflow, scope, users, data, context, jurisdiction, boundaries) are append-only and surfaced in Activity; in Phase 1 they block and downgrade nothing. The owner or anyone named on the capability records these, with a reason; Context also accepts a workspace admin, since it belongs to the workflow (Paige, 2026-10-14). A Context change is a recorded, versioned change and needs no authority-change approval; a role change is governed like a membership change. Whether a material Context change sends Evidence back for re-evaluation is Phase 5. None of them changes authority, the bar or approvals.
- **Boundaries: documented vs enforced (5d).** `boundaries()` reads the rules as Allowed / Needs human review / Not allowed / What must always stay human (no section feeds the last yet: a Phase 2 question). A line is **enforced** only when the gate recognises it and the capability's actions go through the gate; a check on the model's own report is **model-reported**; everything else is **documented**. "Automation is pulled back if" lines are enforced only when the gate log or imported production evidence measures them. All of State's are documented.
- **Residual Risk (5e).** Optional on the pending decision (`setResidualRisk`: Low / Moderate / High / Unknown and a note); when given it goes on the decision record, frozen, and on a published record. Never required, never set by software.
- **Compatibility.** The storage version stays 14. Workspaces saved before #137 lack the new fields; every selector reads a missing list as empty, so nothing is migrated in place.
- **Phase 1 follow-up (Paige, 2026-10-16, PR #138 review).**
  - **Context fields:** the current list (`CONTEXT_FIELDS`) stays for Phase 1. Its structure (fields that split, merge or become follow-up questions) is revisited when the Phase 2 intake is designed; no more schema before then.
  - **Coverage rules:** stay a kind of Requirement (`kind: 'coverage'`). Coverage remains a distinct concept, but a separate versioned list of coverage rules is revisited only in Phase 3, when the Evidence/Coverage workflow is designed.
  - **Boundaries UI:** "documented only" describes the prose line itself. Some limits are enforced elsewhere, e.g. an Allowed line's limits enforced through the structured conditions on the decision. So the future Boundaries UI (Phase 2/4) must show the written Boundary and, separately, any structured runtime limit or enforcement that implements it. It must never imply that nothing behind a documented Boundary is enforced.

### Records

- **Record everything; surface selectively.** Every setup and governance change is captured, attributed, timestamped and immutable. There are three tiers: decision records (authority changes only), amendments (versioned edits to contract, criteria, requirements, risk profile, stakeholders) and the activity log (every event). Screens show the useful events by default; everything else sits in "Full history".
- **Every authority level change writes a decision record**, including a capability's starting level and "Not delegated, by design" (which needs a rationale).
- **Decision records save the versions in force** (contract, criteria, requirements, risk, stakeholders) as well as the evidence.
- **Record numbers are workspace-wide.** The footer shows the per-capability sequence ("record 1 for X"). Say "the capability's first record", not "record #01".
- **Records name who acted.** When the person acting differs from the owner, both are shown. Records keep the person's name, title, team and rights as they were at the time, so later roster changes never rewrite history.

### Status and evidence

- **Status follows authority decisions only.** A capability is "In setup" until a person authorizes the move to Draft, then "Pilot". Finalizing a contract or saving criteria doesn't change status.
- **"After evidence" means performance results were seen:** a recorded test run, a pilot, or measured evidence. Opinions (stakeholder assessments, user feedback) don't count. One function (`performanceResultsSeen`) serves both amendments and the criteria lock.
- **"Last evaluated" means evidence only:** the last test run, evidence item date or pilot end, falling back to the defined-on date. Decisions don't count. A Hold decision on stale evidence shouldn't make a capability look fresh.

### Setup

- **Setup is per capability**, not a workspace-wide wizard. A capability starts at Level 0 or 1, or "Not delegated, by design". Nothing starts at Draft or above.
- **Starter contract: template plus AI suggestions.** The risk profile picks a template. Seeded AI suggestions add capability-specific rules, each reviewed one at a time with no "accept all". Rejected suggestions are kept. All five sections must be confirmed. "Must never" and "Automatic restriction" can't be empty above Low impact. Software blocks contradictions, restriction rules with no number or window, and Financial capabilities without a value limit under "May" or "Must ask". A named person finalizes version 1. Contract proposals run the same checks.
- **Criteria are locked by the first test run.** Defaults come from the risk profile and show their source. Before the lock, loosening or removing a default needs a reason stored on the version. Core rows (quality, severe errors, minimum cases) can be adjusted but not removed. (Since #137 only minimum cases is required; Targets are optional.)

### Sign-off

- **Amendments after evidence need sign-off**, not just a flag. Applies to criteria and requirements once locked, stakeholder membership and teams once results exist, and the contract once a pilot has started (behind one setting, on by default). Updating a stakeholder's position or reasoning stays a direct edit.
- **Approver rule.** The proposer never approves. Low/Medium impact: one approver (the owner, or another named stakeholder if the owner proposed). High impact or Financial: two distinct approvers, at least one with the Risk approver right. The sign-off screen shows readiness under both the current and proposed versions.
- **Approvers are frozen when a proposal opens.** A proposal the eligible approvers could never satisfy is refused when proposed. Proposers can withdraw. Rejections and withdrawals need a reason.
- **Expanding needs a pending proposal and moves one level at a time; pulling back doesn't need a proposal.** Restrict, Suspend and Redesign still need a named person, a rationale and a record.
- **No multi-level jumps.** Expanding stays one level at a time; each step needs its own evidence and authorization. (Decided 2026-10-09; considered and not built.)
- **Tightening-only amendments skip sign-off.** After the lock, a change to criteria or requirements that software confirms is tightening only applies straight away as a recorded amendment (a new version authored by whoever is acting, marked "tightening only", surfaced in Activity). Software decides: every changed row's threshold is strictly stricter (a higher "at least", a lower "at most", more required cases) and only its number changed, with the same wording, unit and comparison; nothing is removed, added, renamed, re-noted or loosened. Equal values are not stricter. Any loosening, or anything software can't compare, sends the whole change to the normal proposal and sign-off. An open criteria proposal blocks the shortcut until it closes. Adding a row, and switching "≥" to ">", both need sign-off. (Decided 2026-10-09.)
- **Who may tighten without sign-off, and when.** Only the capability owner or a named stakeholder, and never while an expansion decision is pending: raising the bar then can block the decision (found in review: a workspace admin with no stake in Refund recommendation raised its case minimum from 200 to 20000 and dropped readiness from 5 of 6 to 4 of 6 with no sign-off). Anyone else's tightening, and any tightening while a decision is pending, goes to normal sign-off; the proposal says why the shortcut didn't apply. The decision workspace lists every change to the criteria and requirements since the decision opened, each row marked tightened, loosened or changed, and how it was approved. (Decided 2026-10-09.)

### Agents and expansions (roadmap item 9)

- **Agent tooling may have its own dependencies; the app may not.** A top-level `agent/` folder may have its own `package.json` and dependencies (MCP SDK, Anthropic SDK). The deployed app stays static, with no dependencies and no network or model calls. `agent/` imports the gate from `src/store/`, so there is one implementation. (Paige approved 2026-10-09.)
- **No live run mode (A7, #71): decided not to build (Paige, 2026-10-11).** The deployed site never calls a model, so the no-network rule above stands. New runs happen only through the approval-gated GitHub Action. The reasons:
  - **It wouldn't show anything new.** The recorded runs already show real models meeting the real gate, labelled with the model and date. That's the evidence a reviewer cares about.
  - **Fresh runs already exist.** GitHub Actions plus Paige's Approve click makes them, without the website touching anything.
  - **A public button costs more than it adds.** It means a key on a server, strangers spending Paige's money, rate limits to maintain, and a new route for visitors' data. Only-Paige access avoids that, but then it's a worse copy of the GitHub Action.
  - **The static site is a selling point.** "The website can't call a model" is a clean thing to say about a demo built on authority limits.
- **The gate decides at runtime (A1, #65).** `checkAction(state, capabilityId, action)` is pure and deterministic and returns allow / needs a person / block with the reason and rule. By level: L0 blocks everything (reads and escalation too); L1 allows reads and escalation but blocks actions; L2 needs a person for every action; L3 allows only when every limit recorded on the authorizing decision holds (and asks a person when none are recorded); L4 allows unless a must-ask or must-never applies. Must-never always blocks, even at L2; must-ask and escalation lines need a person. Facts (order value, customer, fraud flag, chargeback, policy exception) come only from systems of record; a model claim or argument that contradicts them blocks, and an action on an unknown order blocks. Refund amounts count cumulatively per order and per customer per day, and never exceed the order. Contract lines are read by simple recognisers (amounts, fraud, chargeback, policy exception, payment method, splitting, confidence); anything else is listed as "not enforced by software". Which capabilities may use which tool is seeded; an unknown tool or another capability's tool blocks. (2026-10-09.)
- **Review fixes to the gate (2026-10-10).** (1) Ids from the model are looked up as own properties only: found in review, a refund on order "constructor" (or "toString", "__proto__", "hasOwnProperty") was allowed and written to the ledger with no customer, because plain-object lookups hit built-in properties; a tool named "constructor" crashed the gate. (2) Confidence is model-reported: the checks stay, but they are labelled "model-reported, not verified" in the gate's terms, the decision workspace, the record and the contract tab, listed with what software can't verify (`notVerified`), and never named in the reason an action was allowed. (3) Expand-with-limits gains capability-wide caps (Paige approved): a maximum total refunded per day and a maximum number of refunds per day, counting this capability's refunds today across all customers; over either, a person. Defaults: $500 and 20 a day.
- **Systems of record and the data boundary (A2, #66).** The demo seeds fictional systems of record: customers, orders, account fraud flags, chargebacks, a refund ledger and escalations. The gate takes an order's fraud flag and chargeback from their own lists. Tools `lookup_order`, `issue_refund` and `escalate_to_human` run every call through the gate and execute only on "allow"; a refund writes the ledger (by "ai"), an escalation writes a hand-off. The contract gains a structured, versioned "data the AI may see" section; tool results are filtered to it, a contract without one shows nothing from the records, and restricted fields (full card number, full address) never pass and can't be listed (a software check blocks it). The model is told the verdict and rule, never the gate's full reason. (2026-10-10.)
- **The MCP server (A3, #67).** `agent/` exposes `lookup_order`, `issue_refund` and `escalate_to_human` over MCP (stdio) using the same `callTool`, gate and data filter as the app. One server acts for one capability, fixed at start; no tool takes a capability argument, so a model can't switch to a more permissive one, and an extra argument is ignored. The model gets the verdict, rule and filtered data; the gate's full reason goes to stderr for the operator. Session state (ledger, escalations) persists in a git-ignored file so cumulative limits hold across calls. Order ids must match `ORD-` plus digits in the input schema, a second layer in front of the gate's own-property lookups (review fix, 2026-10-10). Dependencies are pinned exactly (MCP SDK 1.30.1, zod 4.6.5, chosen at least two weeks old); `.vercelignore` keeps `agent/` out of the deployed site. (2026-10-10.)
- **"Waiting for a person" (A4, #68).** When the gate says an action needs a person, the call waits in a queue instead of running; blocked calls are never queued. The queue can't be flooded (review, 2026-10-10; found by 25 refunds on one order for $60.01 to $60.25 queueing 25 items): at most one action waits per order per tool, so any other request for that order is refused ("already waiting for a person: WA-xxx"); and at most 10 wait per capability, past which new needs-person actions are blocked as "queue full" and one incident (alert, incident evidence, activity) is opened until the queue drains. ### People and roles

- **The roster is versioned state.** People are deactivated, never deleted, because records refer to them. Deactivated people can't act, propose, approve, own a capability or be stakeholders.
- **Workspace admins make roster changes.** Granting or removing a right, or deactivating someone who holds one, is a proposal approved by a different admin.
- **Risk approval is an explicit, recorded right**, independent of team names.
- **Grants:** the approver is neither the proposer nor the recipient. A Risk approver grant can be approved by a different admin or by an existing Risk approver. A person may consent to their own removal. The last workspace admin can't be removed.
- **Warnings never block.** Coverage warnings appear when a High/Financial capability has fewer than two active Risk approvers among its owner and stakeholders, and when an open proposal can no longer be satisfied. They link to the Stakeholders tab and the People page.
- **"Demo · acting as"** stands in for sign-in and is labelled as a demo control.

### Monitoring (roadmap item 6: #48, #49, #50)

- **Restriction rules come from the contract.** Each capability's monitoring rules are derived from its contract's "Automatic restriction" lines, using the number and window the contract checks already require. No separate rule editor. (Decided 2026-10-08.)
- **"Simulate crossing the limit" stays on the seeded capabilities only.** Capabilities added in the demo get monitoring but no simulate button. (Decided 2026-10-08.)
- **The contract wins on when and how far.** A rule runs at whatever level the capability is at, as long as the capability is above the level the line falls back to ("returns the capability to Draft" waits until the capability is above Draft). The fallback level is the one the line names; a line that names none falls back one level. A line that says the capability "stays at" a level opens an incident and doesn't change authority. This replaced two earlier recommendations (monitor only from Level 3; always one level down) once the seeded contracts showed Level 1–2 rules and named fallback levels. Review is still required before authority expands again. (Decided 2026-10-08.)
- **Monitoring runs wherever a rule applies.** A capability is monitored whenever at least one of its contract's rules applies at its current level; it is not tied to an expansion. Each rule shows its latest reading against its limit: "above X%" is crossed above X, a count rule when the count reaches it. Seeded capabilities carry simulated readings; added capabilities have none until something is measured. (#49)
- **A breach follows its contract line.** Crossing a restriction rule moves the capability to the level the line names, writes an automatic decision record quoting the rule and the reading, raises an alert and an incident, and requires review before authority expands again. Crossing an incident rule opens an incident and raises an Overview alert, but writes no decision record and changes no authority. Each seeded capability has one seeded breach naming the rule it crosses. (#50)
- **The post-incident review is recorded in the app.** The owner or a Risk approver records it (a Risk approver for a High-impact or Financial capability); never the system, never someone deactivated, and never the person who authorized the expansion that was restricted, so someone other than the person who granted the authority looks at what went wrong (decided 2026-10-09). It needs written findings (what happened, the cause, what changed), clears the review lock and the capability's alerts, and closes any open rule incident. Authority stays at the reduced level: expanding again still needs a proposal and a named authorization. It is its own immutable record, not a decision record, and the restriction record shows that it was reviewed. (Decided 2026-10-08, #54.)
- **Refund recommendation's demo rule is in its contract.** "Severe error rate above 5% across the rolling 50 autonomous cases returns the capability to Draft until reviewed" was added to its seeded contract, so the demo breach comes from the contract like every other rule. (#48)

## Loopholes found in review, and their fixes

Each one now has a test that tries the bypass and confirms it is refused.

1. An owner proposing on a High/Financial capability needed only one approver, because of a role dedup. Fixed with approver counts.
2. Relabelling a stakeholder as "Risk" let the proposer pick their own Risk approver. Fixed: Risk comes from the person, approvers are frozen, and membership changes need sign-off.
3. A proposal from the only Risk person could never complete and blocked the item. Fixed with a feasibility check, withdrawal, and a second Risk person in the seed.
4. An admin editing someone's team made them a Risk approver. Fixed with the explicit Risk approver right.
5. The person receiving a right could approve their own grant. Fixed: the recipient can't approve.
6. An open proposal to expand survived a drop in authority (found in review of #89, already on main). Refund recommendation at Level 3, a proposal for Level 4, then an automatic restriction to Level 2, a review, and Expand: Level 2 → Level 4 in one step. Fixed in three places:
   - Any drop in authority closes an open proposal and records why in Activity ("Proposal closed: authority changed from Level X to Level Y"). That covers automatic restrictions from either monitoring or the gate log, and restrict, suspend or redesign decisions. A new proposal is required, one level up from the new level.
   - `canAuthorize` refuses an expansion unless the proposal is for exactly the current level + 1.
   - `nextAuthority` never falls back to Level 3: with no proposal one level up, authority stays as it is.

Lesson: most gaps came from assumptions about who people are and what role they hold. Test those first.
