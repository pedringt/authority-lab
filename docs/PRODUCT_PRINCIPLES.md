# Authority Lab product principles

## Human judgment stays explicit
AI may suggest, summarize, organize, compare and explain. Humans configure consequential choices and grant authority.

## Adaptive presentation, deterministic authority
Generative UI may decide what information and controls are most relevant to surface for the current task. It may not change authority, thresholds, permissions, approval rules, evidence or decision records.

## Technical depth is available, not imposed
Default views use plain language. Operator and technical detail is available through drill-downs.

## Design for domain experts who may not be AI experts
The operator may be a PM, engineer, lawyer, operations lead, consultant or client-side owner. The product must not require formal AI-governance expertise to use responsibly.

## Complex underneath, simple at the point of use
Show only what the user needs for the decision they are making now. Use progressive disclosure for deeper configuration, provenance and technical detail.

## Templates reduce setup effort, not responsibility
Templates may provide structure, questions, examples and suggestions. They must not silently establish risk, boundaries, thresholds, ownership or authority.

## Guidance should accelerate understanding, not replace it
AI help should explain why something matters, what evidence supports a suggestion and what the user can do next.

Do not offer bulk acceptance for consequential AI-generated configuration.

## Evidence gaps are not failures
Missing, stale or insufficient evidence must be visually and conceptually distinct from failed evidence.

## Context makes the product smarter about what to ask
Context may trigger relevant questions and suggestions. It must not silently answer them for the user.

## Authority is capability-specific
Authority belongs to a specific capability, not to an AI system as a whole.

## No authority change without accountability
Every capability has one Owner. No authority change may be approved without one.

## Impact never overrides safety
Time savings, cost savings or adoption do not justify ignoring risk, failed requirements or insufficient evidence.

## Every core workflow has a lightweight path
A team must be able to reach and record a defensible authority decision without enterprise software. Every core evidence and governance workflow should support a native manual, upload or lightweight path.

Integrations improve convenience, automation and confidence. They do not unlock the basic product.

If Authority Lab claims a Boundary is technically enforced, that claim must come from real enforcement evidence or integration. A documented/manual Boundary must not be presented as technically enforced.

## Stay centered on authority decisions
Authority Lab is not primarily an AI inventory, compliance dashboard, vendor-risk system or observability platform. Those systems may feed it. The product remains centered on what a capability should be allowed to do, why, and what evidence would justify changing that permission.

## Material changes can invalidate evidence
Changes to models, prompts, tools, permissions, workflow, scope, users, data or operating context may reduce the relevance of earlier Evidence. Monitoring should make this visible and trigger re-evaluation rather than silently carrying old evidence forward.

## Provider-neutral by design
Authority Lab may use one model provider in the prototype, but the product model must not assume a specific vendor. Model/provider/version belong in technical provenance and evaluation.

## Education through use
Authority Lab should teach good AI decision-making through the workflow without requiring users to study governance first.

## Friendly for presentation, deep enough for investigation
A PM should be able to present the default view to nontechnical stakeholders. Engineers and technical users should be able to inspect detailed evidence, mappings, versions and provenance when needed.
